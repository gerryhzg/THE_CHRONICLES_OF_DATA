# Character voices

Each character wants something beyond explaining a mechanic.

| Character | Motivation and worry | Voice, humor, and emotional beats | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Professor Lyra** | Protect her village; fears the Archive she served is failing people. | Warm mentor: measured sentences, invitations, dry wit. Let hesitation reveal worry before practical help. | “The Archive taught me to keep good records. Apparently it needs reminding.” |
| **Kai** | Prove his instincts; fears falling behind his friend. | Short, energetic challenges and boasting. Jokes land on himself. Disappointment becomes a question or revised plan. | “There goes the victory pose. I'd practiced the eyebrows and everything.” |
| **Professor Inferra** | Understand the forest; worries that overlooked habitats mean overlooked creatures. | Practical scientist: concrete observations, precise questions, gentle deadpan. Correct methods without embarrassment; delight in discoveries. | “Excellent notes. Now, what was happening beyond your notebook?” |
| **Elder Rowan** | Keep neighbors fed and the village united; fears abandoning the system generations relied on. | Plain, measured sentences. Defends familiar practice, then changes policy when records warrant investigation. Never becomes a caricature of stubbornness. | “The Oracle said the fields would be dry. We believed it.” |
| **Farmer Mara** | Save the damaged harvest and protect her neighbors' food. | Direct and practical. Names the consequence before the idea; affection shows in attention to plants and people. | “Water is flowing through the garden channel again.” |
| **Tower Keeper Orin** | Preserve the originals so observations can be checked. | Patient, specific, quietly fond of old instruments and margins. Points to the same cycle and day before drawing a conclusion. | “Rain on the paper, zero rainfall in each copy.” |
| **Shopkeeper Nell** | Keep supplies moving while the village recovers. | Brisk, welcoming, gently amused by her own filing. Makes labels and prices clear. | “A tower emblem beats my filing system, apparently.” |
| **Fisher Bram** | Keep people away from a dangerous swollen river. | Unhurried, grounded in years of watching water. Gives safe, visible directions. | “The river does not read forecasts.” |
| **Sanctuary Keeper Elin** | Help people care for their companions. | Warm, matter of fact, never scolds a defeated player. Makes recovery and the borrowed Cloudfin's return feel ordinary. | “Adventuring is hard work for a pair of feet, or four.” |
| **Village Guard Tomas** | Protect the road while respecting the council's decision. | Clear and courteous. Explains the current prerequisite and where to go next, then welcomes returning travelers. | “The gate stays open. Come home whenever you need a safe rest.” |
| **Village Child** | Recover a scarf and make sense of soggy boots. | Concrete, candid, brief. Humor comes from the visible contradiction. | “The Oracle said no rain! So why are my boots full of water?” |
| **Forest Ranger** | Keep travelers safe; fears missing danger. | Capable, earnest, concrete. Overgeneralizes his night patrols; surprise becomes curiosity and willing revision, never humiliation. | “I know these night paths. Looks like I still owe the daylight a visit.” |
| **Observer** | Help home and bond with companions; worries about deciding without enough information. | Short, active questions and personal reactions. Show understanding through noticing, trying, and revising. | “Then who did we miss while we were asleep?” |
| **DataDex / narrator** | Orient the player and preserve observations. | Concise, sensory, occasionally wry. Acknowledge victories without constant praise or promises about uncertain outcomes. | “Wet grass. Bright tail. Voltkit has noticed you noticing.” |

## Shared dialogue rules

- Start with a character's need or a visible event. Show concrete evidence before naming the concept it illustrates.
- Preserve the facts: forecast **0 mm**, measured **46 mm**; the ranger recorded **18 Bayowl and 12 Samplet**, in the **canopy at night**. The investigation covers **three habitats at two times**, making **six habitat/time records**, not six individual creatures.
- Act I's historical originals record **12 mm in Cycle 62/Day 124**, **28 mm in Cycle 61/Day 126**, and **7 mm in Cycle 60/Day 127**. Each Archive copy says **0 mm**. Keep those past observations separate from the current storm. The mismatch does not establish a culprit, deliberate tampering, or a complete explanation for the failed forecast.
- Preserve Rowan and Lyra's four opening lines: “The Oracle said the fields would be dry. We believed it.” / “Then we need to understand why it was wrong.” / “But the Oracle has never been wrong.” / “That's exactly what worries me.”
- Observed frequencies describe observations. Neither a narrow sample nor a broader one guarantees future behavior or certainty about the whole forest.
- Keep hints actionable: farm crate, river reeds, three shop labels, then Weather Tower comparison; later meadow, riverbank, canopy, daylight and moonlight. The northern gate opens after the council. The forest campfire changes time. Humor must not obscure the next action.
- Write one readable segment at a time. Favor exchanges over speeches, responsive corrections over quiz lectures, and curiosity over shaming.
- Match portrait expressions to the emotional beat: `neutral`, `thoughtful`, `happy`, or `concerned`; only Kai also supports `determined`. Keep names and speaker IDs consistent with `dist/portraits.js`.
